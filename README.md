# Electrostatic Fog Harvester  
### Climate-Resilient Water Access System  

---

## Background

During a winter trek in Mukteshwar, Uttarakhand, I noticed something strange.

We were walking through dense fog. Moisture was everywhere.  
A local guide told us that in winters, water becomes scarce. Streams dry up. Villages struggle.

That did not make sense.

There was water in the air, yet people nearby had none to use.

That moment led to a simple question:

If fog exists, why is water still a problem?

---

## Problem

- Glacier loss is reducing freshwater sources  
- Many regions face chronic water scarcity  
- Fog remains an unused resource  
- Traditional fog nets rely on wind  
- Flat and low-wind areas receive little output  

---

## Solution

A solar-powered electrostatic fog harvester that actively attracts water droplets.

The system creates an electric field to pull droplets from the air.  
This removes dependence on wind and increases collection efficiency.

---

## How It Works

### 1. Solar Charging  
A 100W solar panel charges a 12V battery.  
Fully off-grid operation.

### 2. Pulse Generation  
A 555 timer generates pulses:  
- 1 kHz frequency  
- 20% duty cycle  

### 3. High Voltage Step-Up  
A DC-DC converter boosts 12V to ~20,000V DC.  
Pulsed operation reduces heat and energy loss.

### 4. Electrostatic Field  
Thin wires create a field above a grounded mesh.  
Fog droplets get polarized within 20 to 30 cm.

### 5. Droplet Attraction  
Charged droplets move toward the mesh.

### 6. Coalescence  
Droplets merge and grow.  
Hydrophobic coating prevents sticking.

### 7. Collection  
Water drips into a collection tray.  
Stored for use.
